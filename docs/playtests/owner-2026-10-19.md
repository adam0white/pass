## Where it was fun

- **Thursday to Saturday.** Input density tripled once the rail filled: 19, 27 and 17 asks, cooks pulled between grill, cold and dish. On Saturday 12 tickets were open at 20:06 and four tables walked. The owner insisted twice at that peak. This is the time-management classic working.
- **Friday's call.** Dilek's mother fell; the owner let her go at once. Her evening line was "You let me go to her. I won't forget it.", and her own review was "I keep going back over going to my mother."
- **Rafi's jar.** He skimmed ₺24 on Wednesday; his review said "I didn't keep my rule about the jar. Next time I'll be ready when it comes." The owner moved the jar to the pass.
- **Managing bodies.** The owner sent cooks to eat eight times and to take ten three times, before they asked. That is the "better simulated humans" twist being played.

## Where it went flat

1. **Nobody said no.** Of 92 asks, 88 were answered "Fine." (44) or "Alright, I'll do it." (43). There were two deferrals ("Not now. After I take ten out back.", "Not now. After I have a cigarette in the yard."), two counter-offers from Rafi ("I'll wash plates instead.") and no refusal all week. The game's promise, that every cook can say no, showed up four times.
2. **Insisting had no price.** All three insists went to a cook whose button already said "yes". Trust ended where it started, so the evening never said "You pushed me".
3. **The evening repeated.** HF's kept-day line ("Today I kept maghrib and my shift.") is filtered out, so on most nights only the game's two-line pools spoke. Selim said "A long night. You were there for it." three times, Dilek said "My feet are gone. Good service, though." three times, and Rafi said "Is it always like this? Don't answer." three times.
4. **Too easy for a first week.** Monday and Tuesday lost nothing. On Tuesday the owner made no input for the first 134 minutes and still fed 20 of 20.
5. **The jar story ended without a word.** Once the jar moved to the pass, Rafi had no temptation, no line and no consequence for the rest of the week.
6. **Three cooks out back at once:** 14 minutes on Tuesday, 27 on Wednesday, 13 on Thursday. The asks show the cost: 25 of 92 went to cooks on a break or eating, mostly "back to the grill".
7. **It ends on Saturday.** "I need more."

## What was unclear

- Rafi's prompting printed HF's raw lens vocabulary three times on Wednesday: "Rafi glanced at the jar. The self pulled; held back." It reads as jargon.
- Dilek's call: the owner tapped "Go. Now." twice in the same minute (two `call` inputs at 19:44), because the first tap showed nothing. Dilek then answered being released with "After I work the cold side, then I will." and "Alright, I'll do it.", HF's generic deferral and assent lines, which are wrong for a woman whose mother is on the floor.
- 17 asks came with no ticket on the rail: early-evening asks answered "Fine." to nothing.
- Insisting on a "yes" looks the same as insisting on a "no". The button does not say that insisting on a yes only adds pressure.

## Reported bug: a break blocks one job but not another

The owner, verbatim (relayed by the coordinator): "The restaurant management, it might be a bug, someone is taking a break and they wouldn't accept a task, but they accept other tasks, after which they accept the original task anyway, like a workaround."

**Reproduced** on the live build (3c1faa9) headlessly, seed 1, Monday 19:24, Selim one minute into "take ten out back":

| Ask | Verdict | Selim says | Deciding numbers |
|---|---|---|---|
| grill | deferred | "Not now. After I take ten out back." | grill 0.093 (with `forecast:body` -0.149) against the running break 0.029 plus inertia (up to 0.15) |
| cold | assented | "Alright, I'll do it." | cold 0.224, no forecast cost, clears the break's inertia |
| grill, two minutes later, from the cold side | assented | "Alright, I'll do it." | grill 0.105 against cold, the chef's own request, which keeps no inertia against the chef |

The same pattern occurs on every seed checked: it is how decisions resolve, not a one-off.

**Cause: both, mostly the explanation.** The verdicts follow HF's rules: a running activity the person chose keeps a switching margin (HF `will/`, `switchMargin`), and the grill's body forecast over the rest of the rush makes it the one job that cannot clear it. The incoherence is in what the cook says. HF's deferral line names the counter-offer ("after I take ten"), so the break sounds like the reason, when the reason is the grill's cost against the break. Once he is off the break and working, the grill is a fair ask. A person would say so. HF does not report which term decided a deferral, so a host cannot phrase it. That is filed in [docs/hf-requests.md](../hf-requests.md). On the host side the game cleared the deferral as soon as another ask came in, and so lost the thread.

**Fix (host, in pass@2):** when a cook defers a station because of a break they are taking, the answer says what is different about this job ("Off my break for the grill? Let me have my ten. The cold side I'd do."), naming only the jobs HF predicts they would take now. The deferral is remembered for the rest of that break. When they later take the deferred job after all, because they are up and working anyway, they say that ("I'm up now anyway. The grill, fine."). Test: `test/game.test.ts`, "a break refusal stays coherent across asks".
