**1. Verdict**

I'd keep playing this, mostly for the ledger and the back step. The people are the best part. When they react to something specific I did, it works. But the lines repeat too much, and some reactions don't match what happened. And by week 2 the money pressure felt like a stress timer. You told me there wouldn't be one.

**2. Where it moved me, where it went flat, what was unclear**

What moved me:
- **Wednesday, week 1.** The tip jar came up lighter than it should have been. Rafi said, "I didn't keep my rule about the jar." I picked "sit with Rafi on the back step." The journal said, "I sat with Rafi on the back step. I did not say the word thief." Then he said, "When I look at the jar, I go scrub the trays. Okay, Chef." That's the best writing in the game. It's kind, and it gave him something to do instead.
- **Friday, week 1, 19:40.** Dilek said, "That was my neighbour. Mum's had a fall. Chef, can I go?" I said yes. The food writer walked out at 20:18 ("One of them had a notebook"). The kindness cost me something real, and I didn't mind. Afterwards she said, "You let me go to her. I won't forget it." A week later her mum came back: the phone call, then "She'd fallen asleep with the television on," then "Mum asked if you're married. I said I don't know." That thread is lovely.
- **Thursday, week 2.** The game said Rafi had been quiet all afternoon. Then he asked, "can I have fifty of Saturday's money now? My sister's school trip." The next night he said, "My sister's going on the trip. Because of you." He also put an extra börek in Ilyas's bag, the way Nevin used to.
- **Sunday, week 3, Nevin's ledger.** "Gül Arslan — since the boy's father. Pide for the grandson, always." I had already sent that boy home hungry twice: "Mrs Arslan took the boy home hungry." The ledger landed right on top of that guilt. It was a real punch. I forgave her tab.
- **Zeynep's first night.** Her answers showed as "yes? (a guess, in pencil)." That's a perfect way to show you don't know someone yet.
- **The regulars.** Hasan Bey asked "if the lentils were still Nevin's." The Aksoy sisters argued about Nevin's börek. These made the street feel like a place.

Where it went flat:
- **The service evenings.** They were mostly me watching Selim go "out back" while the grill backed up. The cooks wander. Selim kept drifting to the cold side or the dish pit with grill tickets waiting, three or four times a night. Most nights around 17:55 to 18:15, three cooks stood at the dish pit together. The Sunday page said, "Selim goes to the grill without being told now," but on the line I watched him walk away from it.
- **The oven.** I never understood it. It goes from "cold" to "warming" to "hot," but it seemed to cool whenever the cook left, and the cooks kept leaving. Rafi's jar habit ("looked at the jar, then went and scrubbed the trays") pulled him off the oven every 20 to 25 minutes. Pide almost never came out, and that's why the boy went hungry.
- **Customers leaving silently.** Kemal ("in late and in a hurry") vanished from the rail twice with no line about it. Ilyas left hungry on Saturday of week 3 and nobody said a word. The friday birthday that was announced never showed up as anything I could see.

What was unclear:
- **"jar talk" during service** did nothing visible on Tuesday or Wednesday. Then Rafi spoke of "my rule about the jar," a rule I never saw him make.
- **"answer yes" to Rafi's börek request** after close gave no confirmation at all.
- **The calendar.** After each Sunday the button said "Open on Wednesday." Monday and Tuesday of weeks 2 and 3 just never happened.
- **The bill.** The landlord's amount crept up every night: ₺4,200, then 4,270, 4,340, 4,410, 4,480. I guessed it was Zeynep's wages. Nothing on the screen said so.
- **"Selim kept to what was said last night."** I never saw what was said.

**3. Did the cooks feel like people? Did they remember? Repeats and false notes**

They remember the big things, and those were moving: the jar rule, the mum, the school trip, the "five minutes." The small things repeat, and some lines are false.

Repeats:
- Selim: "Nevin used to call it the same way. Almost." (about five times)
- Selim: "The lamb was good tonight. Tell Ahmet." (three times)
- Selim: "A long night. You were there for it." (twice)
- Rafi: "Rafi looked at the jar, then went and scrubbed the trays." (every 20 to 25 minutes, every night, for two weeks; one evening the journal showed it three lines in a row)
- Every cook, every time I tapped them: "I'll still have enough for the rest of the rush."

Lines that rang false:
- **Rafi: "You asked about the oven and let it go. Nobody does that."** He said it three nights. On those nights I had asked him for the oven and he went, and once I insisted.
- **Rafi's earlier version: "You asked about the grill and let it go."** He actually did go to the grill ten minutes later.
- **Dilek: "I said not now and you let me."** That was the night I insisted she take the grill.
- **"I didn't keep X. Next time I'll be ready when it comes."** I can see the template.
  - Selim said it about maghrib, though he stepped into the storeroom for maghrib every single night.
  - Zeynep said "I didn't keep being home for my boys" before she had worked one shift.
  - She said it again the night I let her leave early for her son's fever.
- **Dilek: "You took my no at 19:15. I'll take your yes another night."** It came back a week later word for word, only the time changed to 19:55. Quoting the exact minute sounds like a log file, not a person.
- **Dilek's "Five minutes, Chef"** lasted more than an hour.
- **Dilek: "Thank you. Thank you, Chef."** It was followed at once by "Alright, I'll do it." That generic line, right after a heartfelt moment, sounded like a vending machine.
- **"A new face on the line tonight, if I hired one."** This appeared the night after Zeynep had already worked a shift.

**4. Stress and money**

Week 1 was fine. I paid the bank's ₺5,200 with ₺400 to spare. Saturday's "Whatever is in the till at close is what we have" felt like a story beat, not a timer.

By weeks 2 and 3 the screen became a wall of "(restless)" tickets: 15 of 28 tables fed, then 15 of 33. My fixes were tapping the same three people back to the same stations. That's the stress-timer feeling I play cozy games to avoid. I don't think it was hard choices that wore me out. It was the cooks wandering.

The money did mean something when it was tied to a person:
- I let Dilek go to her mum, and that cost me the food writer.
- Rafi's ₺50 for the school trip.
- The ledger: forgive Gül, or hold Hasan's tab until his pension comes.

Missing the landlord was gentle: "₺945 short. The rest plus a fee is due next Saturday." I liked that it didn't end the game.

**5. The three changes I'd most want**

1. **Make the after-close lines and the journal true to what happened, and stop the repeats.** Never say "you let it go" after I insisted. Give each person a bigger pool of lines, or rest a line after it's used. Collapse Rafi's jar line after the first time each night.
2. **Make wandering readable or make it stop.** Show me why Selim leaves the grill when tickets are waiting. Better, let him keep the habit the Sunday page praises him for. Give the oven a cook who stays and one sentence that explains how it heats.
3. **Let me actually talk to people.** "jar talk" during service and the "Rafi has been quiet" morning both made me want to go and ask. Even one line back would do. And when a named regular leaves hungry (Ilyas, Kemal), say so in the journal the way you do for Mrs Arslan.
